# Archive Doctrine V1

Operation ID: OP-ARCHIVE-OS-FOUNDATION-BLOCKS-A-H-V1

Date: 2026-06-25

## Why Archive Exists

Archive exists to preserve the institutional memory of Mental Smile.

It preserves:

- what happened;
- why it happened;
- what changed;
- what was approved;
- what failed;
- how it was fixed;
- what must be recoverable;
- what must never be silently rewritten.

Archive is not a passive folder. Archive is the permanent memory and recovery layer of the platform.

## What Enters Archive

Archive receives:

- certified constitutions;
- guides;
- cards;
- registries;
- operational reports;
- freeze reports;
- migration records;
- failure records;
- recovery records;
- prompt recovery packages;
- provider portfolio assets;
- marketing content and asset records;
- historical section records;
- operation history;
- era records;
- architectural evolution records.

## What Does Not Enter Archive

Archive does not store:

- credentials;
- secrets;
- passwords;
- raw client data;
- user timelines;
- private notebook text;
- suggestion text unless explicitly archived as governed public/platform material;
- complaint narratives unless legally approved and governed;
- raw analytics;
- ungoverned runtime dumps;
- unapproved marketing media as source of truth.

## Archive Vs History

History is a record of the past.

Archive is governed history.

History can describe what happened.

Archive must also preserve ownership, classification, source, version, evidence, and recovery meaning.

## Archive Vs Registry

A registry lists active or governed items and their relationships.

Archive preserves the full lifecycle, including previous states, retired states, migration records, failures, and recovery packages.

Registry answers:

"What exists and where is it owned?"

Archive answers:

"How did this become true, what evidence supports it, and how can it be recovered?"

## Archive Vs Documentation

Documentation explains systems.

Archive preserves documentation as governed memory.

Documentation may be current-facing.

Archive must preserve current records, historical records, retired records, and source-of-truth lineage.

## Doctrine Rule

No major system memory belongs only to ordinary documentation.

If it affects continuity, ownership, recovery, compliance, or future planning, it belongs in Archive.
